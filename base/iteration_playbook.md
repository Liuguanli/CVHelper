# Iteration Playbook

## Goal

The goal of each iteration is not to make the resume look fancier. The goal is to make it:

- Closer to the target role
- Easier to pass automated screening
- Easier for HR to understand at a glance
- Fully truthful and defensible in interviews

## Core Rule

Do not iterate just for the sake of iterating.
Only revise when you know exactly what this round should improve.

## Best Iteration Order

1. Review `job_analysis_<date>.md`
2. Review `ats_review_<date>.md`
3. Review `hr_review_<date>.md`
4. Then revise:
   - `Profile`
   - The top 2-4 bullets in the most relevant experience
   - `Skills` ordering
   - First paragraph of cover letter
   - First 30 seconds of video script

## What To Improve First

### Round 1: ATS fit

Prioritize:

- Whether key JD terms appear naturally in real experience
- Whether the most relevant experience appears first
- Whether `Profile` is too generic
- Whether skills ordering aligns with role direction

Avoid:

- Keyword stuffing
- Over-claiming unfamiliar technologies

## Round 2: HR clarity

Prioritize:

- Can HR understand your profile in 20 seconds?
- Does it feel too academic, too broad, or mismatched for the role?
- Does the first paragraph of the cover letter map directly to team needs?
- Is the video opening short, clear, and steady?

## Round 3: Research selling

If research is a key strength, prioritize:

- The problem was ...
- I built ...
- The key point was ...
- This led to VLDB 2025 / submitted to VLDB 2026

Do not only list publication names.
Explain problem, method, and impact first; venue second.

## Round 4: Tone control

Check for:

- AI-like phrasing
- Too many vague words
- Over-packaged claims
- Sentences that are too long to read naturally

If found, rewrite into:

- Shorter sentences
- More concrete verbs
- Phrasing that is easy to expand in interviews

## Only Change 3 Things Per Round

Focus on at most three key changes per round:

- One `Profile` issue
- One highest-impact experience issue
- One clear HR/ATS risk

This keeps iteration stable and makes impact measurable.

## Good Questions To Ask Each Round

- Is this statement supported by concrete evidence?
- Is this statement more generic than the JD itself?
- Can I naturally expand this in an interview?
- Does this help ATS, or only sound sophisticated?
- Will HR be clearer after reading it, or more confused?

## When To Stop Iterating

Stop major edits when these are already true:

- ATS review has no obvious hard blockers
- HR review suggests moving to recruiter screen
- `Profile` is highly aligned with the role
- Strongest experience is already placed first
- Language is natural without obvious AI tone

Past this point, heavy edits usually add noise.

## Long-Term Improvement

Real long-term gains come from reusable modules, not endless polishing of one resume:

- database/systems version
- data infrastructure version
- research scientist version
- AI/retrieval version

Each module should include:

- One profile paragraph
- 4-8 core bullets
- 2-4 STAR-style research stories
- One 30-second self-intro

## Recommended Habit

After each application, add one short retrospective:

- Which expressions sounded most like you
- Which expressions best matched the JD
- Which polished lines were not fully authentic
- What can be reused directly next time

This keeps your materials progressively stronger and more consistent.

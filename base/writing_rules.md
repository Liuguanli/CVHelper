# Writing Rules

## Shared Rules

- Everything must remain truthful and defensible in an interview.
- Prefer concrete evidence over vague adjectives.
- Reuse language from the target job description only when it matches real experience.
- If there is a gap, soften it honestly instead of hiding it.

## Resume Tailoring Rules

- Keep edits minimal and targeted.
- Preserve the existing LaTeX structure unless a section truly needs to change.
- Prioritize reordering bullets, tightening wording, and swapping emphasis before adding new content.
- Highlight the most relevant systems, pipelines, backend, data, or AI-retrieval experience for the role.
- Avoid keyword stuffing.
- Do not let the resume sound generic, over-polished, or AI-written.
- Avoid inflated verbs or claims that cannot be defended in an interview.
- Prefer plain, specific, technical wording over vague business language.
- Mirror important JD terminology when it truthfully matches your experience, so the resume stays ATS-friendly.
- Keep enough role-relevant keywords for screening, but anchor each keyword to real evidence from your background.
- If a contribution was collaborative, do not rewrite it as sole ownership.
- Do not add fake metrics, fake scale, or fake impact.

## Cover Letter Rules

- Write in first person.
- Tone should be confident, calm, and conversational.
- Avoid generic phrases like "I am writing to express my interest".
- Keep it specific to the company and role.
- Show fit through 2 to 4 strong examples, not through a long list.

## HR Video Script Rules

- The script should sound natural when read aloud.
- Target spoken length: 3 to 4 minutes.
- Absolute maximum: under 5 minutes.
- Use short sentences and clean transitions.
- Structure:
  1. Who I am
  2. Why this role fits me
  3. Two or three relevant examples
  4. Why I want this company / team
  5. Short closing
- Avoid sounding like a sales pitch.
- End with a simple, human closing.

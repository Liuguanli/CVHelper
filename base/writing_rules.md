# Writing Rules

## Shared Rules

- Everything must remain truthful and defensible in an interview.
- Prefer concrete evidence over vague adjectives.
- Reuse language from the target job description only when it matches real experience.
- If there is a gap, soften it honestly instead of hiding it.

## Resume Tailoring Rules

- Keep edits minimal and targeted.
- Preserve the existing LaTeX structure unless a section truly needs to change.
- Prioritize reordering bullets, tightening wording, and swapping emphasis before adding new content.
- Highlight the most relevant systems, pipelines, backend, data, or AI-retrieval experience for the role.
- Avoid keyword stuffing.
- Do not let the resume sound generic, over-polished, or AI-written.
- Avoid inflated verbs or claims that cannot be defended in an interview.
- Prefer plain, specific, technical wording over vague business language.
- Mirror important JD terminology when it truthfully matches your experience, so the resume stays ATS-friendly.
- Keep enough role-relevant keywords for screening, but anchor each keyword to real evidence from your background.
- If a contribution was collaborative, do not rewrite it as sole ownership.
- Do not add fake metrics, fake scale, or fake impact.
- ATS friendliness should be judged conservatively, not optimistically.
- A strong ATS score requires both keyword overlap and believable evidence behind those keywords.

## Cover Letter Rules

- Write in first person.
- Tone should be confident, calm, and conversational.
- Avoid generic phrases like "I am writing to express my interest".
- Keep it specific to the company and role.
- Show fit through 2 to 4 strong examples, not through a long list.
- When highlighting research ability, do not only name papers or venues.
- Use a light STAR structure:
  - Situation / problem
  - Task / technical challenge
  - Action / what I built or proposed
  - Result / why it mattered
- Mention top venues naturally when they strengthen credibility, for example `VLDB 2025`, `ICDE 2023`, or `submitted to VLDB 2026`.
- If a paper is not yet accepted, say that clearly.
- Explain the key point of the research in simple language, not paper-title language.

## HR Video Script Rules

- The script should sound natural when read aloud.
- Target spoken length: 3 to 4 minutes.
- Absolute maximum: under 5 minutes.
- Use short sentences and clean transitions.
- Preferred opening structure:
  1. Hi, I am Guanli Liu.
  2. Where I am based / where I come from
  3. Which role I am applying for
  4. Why I am applying
- Preferred overall structure:
  1. Who I am
  2. Where I am based
  3. Which role I am applying for
  4. Why I am applying
  5. What I have been working on recently
  6. Why I match this role
  7. What I can bring
  8. Short closing
- Avoid sounding like a sales pitch.
- End with a simple, human closing.
- If talking about research, use short STAR-style examples instead of listing publications.
- Mention a venue only when it adds credibility and can be said smoothly aloud.
- Prefer simple spoken wording such as:
  - I worked on...
  - The problem was...
  - I built...
  - The result was...
- For spoken English, avoid long technical noun chains and hard-to-pronounce abstract words when a simpler phrase works.

## Review Rules

- Each role package should end with two lightweight review artifacts:
  - an ATS / machine-screening review
  - an HR / recruiter review
- ATS scoring should use a 0 to 100 range and stay conservative.
- HR review should sound like a fast recruiter read, not like a technical committee review.
- Both reviews should call out real risks, not just positives.
- If the role has major fit gaps, say so directly instead of smoothing them away.
